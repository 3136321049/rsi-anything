# Sitting at Work All Day? Learn About Physical Activity

This health education piece is for generally healthy adults and introduces the public recommendations of the World Health Organization. It is not a substitute for individual medical advice or a personal exercise assessment.

## The recommendations

For adults aged {{adult_age}}, WHO recommends {{moderate_minutes}} of moderate-intensity aerobic activity a week, or {{vigorous_minutes}} of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity, or an equivalent combination of the two. Muscle-strengthening activity should work the major muscle groups on {{strength_days}} a week. [W1]

## Everyday life

Physical activity does not only happen in a gym; walking, cycling and some everyday activities can all be part of it. A little activity is better than none, and you can start with a small amount you are able to do and increase it gradually. Reducing sedentary time also deserves attention. [W1][W2]

## How to read this

These recommendations describe how much activity to do and what kinds. Walking to work is one example of fitting activity into daily life, but light movement does not automatically count toward the moderate- to vigorous-intensity target. How to arrange it depends on your own situation; this piece gives no exercise prescriptions for patients and does not discuss treating disease. [W2]
